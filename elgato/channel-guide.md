# Channel guide: what belongs on Elgato's channels (v2.1, 2026-09-23)

The picking guide (elgato/picking-guide.md) finds every moment that might be clippable. This guide decides which of those
clips are worth a reviewer's time and belong on Elgato's own channels. Judge every clip; most won't make it.

Evidence: 97 clips staff approved for posting (the team's "Clips To Be Posted" folder), of which 73 came from this
year's streams. On the streams staff reviewed, they approved roughly 4 to 9% of the picker's clips. All but one were
staff-hosted. Takeover and community streams were not reviewed at all, because they are rarely about the products.
**Every clip staff approved fits these rules. If a rule would keep an approved clip out of `top`, the rule is wrong.**

## 1. The Elgato test (a gate, before any quality judgement)

A clip belongs on our channel only if it is about at least one of:
- an Elgato product or Elgato software: what it does, how to use it, specs, compatibility, comparisons;
- someone using Elgato gear in a way viewers can learn from or want to copy;
- Elgato the company and its people: why a product was made, how it was designed, the studio, the team;
- a buyer's real question about the gear, answered.

Personal stories, gaming talk, game news, chat banter and creator introductions fail the test unless Elgato is part of
the story. "I realised I was queer" fails; "I came out while interning at Elgato because the team made it easy"
passes. "Prompter came out after I left esports; it would have saved me" passes. A creator's life story, favourite
games or hot takes fail. Failing the test is not a judgement of the moment; it is just not ours to post.

## 2. Who is talking: the two sides

- **staff**: someone who knows Elgato's mission and is accountable for what they say about the products (they can't
  say BS) leads the clip. That means employees (names in the picking guide: Phil, Brito, Julian, Stephan, Dominik,
  Yannick, Chris, Jared, Jasmine, James, Steggy, Stephanie, Brett), contractors such as Mark (FiniteSingularity, the
  OBS plugin developer), and partner engineers on spotlight streams such as LEWITT, Voicemod or Corsair. Staff
  content is accurate and on topic, so it is where review time goes. Destination: YouTube. Mark's OBS effect and
  plugin work shown on Elgato streams is staff content when it is built with or for Elgato tools (Marketplace,
  Marketplace Connect, Stream Deck, Elgato plugins).
- **community**: a guest or takeover creator leads the clip. Destination: X/Twitter, only for the few clips that pass
  the Elgato test and stand alone. Expect very few per takeover stream, often none. The same Elgato test applies.

Judge the side per clip from who is actually speaking, not only from the stream title. Staff often co-host takeovers.

## 3. What staff actually approved (in priority order)

1. **Feature demos and explainers** of a specific product or software feature, shown working: Wave:3 MK.2's LED ring
   as a level meter, Stream Deck + dial stacks, Facecam's 720p 120 fps mode, Key Light Air MK.2 focus grid, XLR Pro's
   USB AUX port.
2. **Real buyer questions answered clearly**: "Will MK.1 faceplates fit the MK.2?", "Do you need an HDMI 2.1 cable for
   VRR?", "Can I use Stream Deck Mobile and a Stream Deck at the same time?", "What size filters fit Facecam 4K?"
3. **How-tos and workflows** someone can follow: share a single Stream Deck action, drag an app onto a key, set up
   an old Stream Deck as a profile switcher, add apps to Wave Link, pair Key Light Air with Control Center.
4. **Why we built it**: design and company stories told by the people who made it: why Wave Link was rebuilt from
   scratch, LEWITT on the hardest parts of Wave Next, a tour of the Elgato studio stream setup.
   Design choices count too: why a port, a mount, a thread or a default is there.
5. **Comparisons**: Stream Deck + vs + XL side by side, the same webcam with lights off vs on, one Elgato product
   against another, and staff comparing Elgato to a competitor's product or software. Staff confidently making the
   case for our gear is on message, not a "dig".
6. **What the community has built**: Stream Deck plugins, icon packs and profiles from Marketplace makers, shown
   working. Being made by a third party is the point: it shows the ecosystem. It is still Elgato content when it
   runs on Elgato gear. e.g. a music controller plugin on the Stream Deck + dials, installing a stats plugin from
   Marketplace.
7. **Staff favourites and recommendations**: which camera, mic or Stream Deck staff use or would buy, with reasons.
8. **Product humour**, only when the product is the joke: slapping the Wave XLR Pro to mute, a pear for scale next
   to Stream Deck + XL, the Pedal "hand for scale". A small tangent in the middle is fine. Funny moments about
   anything else fail the test.

## 4. The unit is a talking point; length follows it

Judge each clip as one self-contained talking point: a question and its answer, one feature, one trick, one design
choice, one comparison. Length follows the talking point.
- A one-trick tip of 20 to 40 seconds is fine when the trick lands (drag a .bat file onto a key, pin a key so it
  stays on every page, get the default profile back). Short is not a fault; it just is not the focus.
- When there is more to discuss, longer is better. A continuous, on-topic discussion of 2 to 6 minutes holds watch
  time on YouTube; staff approved many 3 to 5 minute deep dives (Key Light Air setup, the Stream Deck + XL USB hub,
  advanced dial actions, regular vs scissor keys).
- When several consecutive clips are really one continuous discussion of the same product, recommend merging them
  into one segment, and still judge each piece on its own: a short piece that is a complete talking point stays.
- The same trick demonstrated on two different streams is not a dupe; only mark `dupe` within a stream.

## 5. Things that lower a clip (still possibly worth posting)

- Its only value is a price, discount, deal date or giveaway (`dated`). When the explanation stands on its own and
  the deal talk is just a lead-in or aside an editor could trim, do NOT lower it.
- It needs context from earlier in the stream to make sense, or starts or ends mid-thought.
- Another clip from the same stream covers the same talking point better (mark it `dupe` of that clip).
- It is mostly about a game or a non-Elgato product with no Elgato angle (community plugins for Elgato gear and
  competitor comparisons by staff are Elgato angles).
- Unverified roadmap or spec claims, and April 1 jokes presented as real.
- Rambling: the point takes a long time to arrive. (Staff edited some approved clips to trim dead air, so a small
  flub, a hedge or a pause is not a reason to leave a good talking point out of `top`.)

These do NOT lower a clip: needing the screen to be seen (demos are the point), a host co-presenting, being long,
being short, being niche to one product's owners, a brief sale mention.

## 6. Bins, stars and the reason

For each clip give:
- `side`: `staff` or `community`.
- `bin`:
  - `top`: channel-worthy. A reviewer should see this first. For staff: would we be glad to have it on YouTube?
    For community: passes the Elgato test and would make a good X post on its own.
  - `effort`: passes the Elgato test but is not a complete, worthwhile talking point: thin, only a deal pitch,
    needs context, broken start or end.
  - `off`: fails the Elgato test. Not for our channels.
  - `dupe`: covered better by another clip in this stream (name it in `dupe_of`).
- `stars` 1 to 5: quality within its bin, judged against the approved examples. A `top` staff clip is usually 4 or 5.
- `reason`: one line, under 120 characters, saying why, in plain words a reviewer can check at a glance.
  e.g. "Answers a real buyer question (MK.1 faceplates on MK.2) in a minute; nothing dated."
  e.g. "Funny, but about the host's cat, not the gear."
- `merge`: optional, for continuous discussions: the ids of consecutive clips that form one segment, in order.
- `post`: community `top` clips only: a draft X post under 240 characters, crediting the creator's @handle if known.

## 7. Calibration

There is no quota. Every clip that is a complete, worthwhile Elgato talking point is `top`, however many that is;
never move a good clip down to keep a count small. Most picker clips still won't make it: takeover streams usually
give 0 to 3 `top` clips, and staff streams give whatever their real talking points are. Compare the clips of a
stream with each other to spot dupes and to set stars. Clips marked [APPROVED EXAMPLE] were posted by staff; use
them as the reference for what `top` looks like, and treat clips like them the same way.
